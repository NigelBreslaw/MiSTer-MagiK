# Generation briefs

All ten images used the `stylized-concept` image-generation mode. Shared
constraints were: a 16:9 MiSTer MagiK realtime particle-effect reference frame,
absolute true-black background, no text or HUD, abundant negative space,
restrained bloom, RGB565-friendly palette separation, and no rendering technique
that would be implausible for a 60 Hz software renderer.

## 01 — Procedural sprite materials

A graceful rising garden of embers, sparks, tiny four-point stars, soft glow
discs, faint smoke puffs, and a few angular glowing shards. Particles evolve
from white-hot cores through gold, amber, magenta, and deep red using visibly
different simple 1–7 pixel sprite silhouettes and quantized softness bands.

## 02 — Variable-width ribbons and streaklets

Several hundred moving heads sweep through an S-shaped three-dimensional
gesture. Only selected particles leave continuous tapered ribbons with
white-hot heads, variable width, bead breakup, controlled halos, and colour
decay through cyan, cobalt, violet, magenta, and sparse gold.

## 03 — Curl-noise flow field

Thousands of tiny particles are advected through broad continuous currents with
smaller nested eddies, including two readable curls and a counter-current.
Bright tracer particles expose the field direction. Motion must read as a
coherent vector field rather than random jitter or a single analytic vortex.

## 04 — Low-resolution density and bloom

Compact streams overlap into a crescent-shaped energy knot. Individual dots
remain visible at the edge while accumulated density forms stepped cyan,
cobalt, violet, and magenta light around dark cavities and bright density
ridges, as if produced by a quarter-resolution accumulation and blur buffer.

## 05 — Layered systems and child emission

Three primary white-gold comet heads arc from a compact source. Each sheds
speed-dependent red and magenta sparks, produces one thin cyan event ring, and
ends in a delicate cobalt and mint child burst. The parent/child hierarchy is
explicit and bounded rather than a conventional radial firework.

## 06 — Spatial field stack

Three separate streams demonstrate attraction, repulsion, and vortex release.
A cyan attractor gathers one stream, a magenta repulsor opens a clean black
cavity, and a gold vortex captures then releases another stream. Falloffs are
shown only through motion, without field guides or diagrams.

## 07 — Depth-aware material LOD

An oblique particle tunnel has large soft magenta foreground sprites, crisp
cyan and cobalt particles on the focal middle plane, and tiny dim mint and
blue-gray distant points. Fast particles become short depth-directed streaks,
with a preserved black travel corridor and coarse but convincing depth layers.

## 08 — Source-driven emission and morph

One continuous point cloud transforms from a classic gold/orange arcade
joystick into a cyan/cobalt game controller. Ordered magenta and violet
assignment paths bridge the two recognizable particle-only silhouettes while
source colours and sparse white feature points remain legible.

## 09 — SDF collision and collision events

A narrow cyan particle waterfall strikes an invisible bowl-shaped signed
distance collider, slides along its surface, and creates bounded splash children
and low mist. A separate warm stream wraps and bounces around an invisible
sphere. Collider shapes are inferred only from particle behavior.

## 10 — Grid-accelerated flocking

Thousands of cyan, cobalt, mint, and violet agents form an abstract murmuration
with alignment, separation, and cohesion. The swarm divides around a clean dark
avoidance cavity, rejoins into two curling wings, and reacts locally to a few
gold chaser particles without becoming a solid body or dense wire mesh.
